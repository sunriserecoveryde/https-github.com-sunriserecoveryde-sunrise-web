PHASE 2 — CORRECT THE DASHBOARD INFORMATION HIERARCHY
==================================================

The dashboard currently has strong information but too many competing elements.

Reorganize it into four clear levels:

LEVEL 1 — IMMEDIATE ACTION
Place the most urgent information at the top:
- Critical clinical alerts
- Withdrawal alerts
- AMA-risk alerts
- Expiring authorizations
- Overdue signatures
- Medication or safety concerns

Use one consolidated “Immediate Action” area instead of multiple competing alert strips.

Each alert must show:
- Severity
- Patient or operational subject
- Reason
- Responsible role or staff member
- Due time
- Primary action button
- Mark reviewed or dismiss option, where permitted

LEVEL 2 — TODAY’S PRIORITIES
Create a concise action summary showing:
- Items needing action today
- Assigned owner
- Deadline
- Link to the exact record or workflow

LEVEL 3 — KEY PERFORMANCE INDICATORS
Keep the primary KPI cards:
- Census
- Occupancy
- AMA risk
- Pending co-signatures
- Average length of stay
- Discharges

Improve them by:
- Using consistent card heights
- Using consistent trend formatting
- Adding tooltip definitions
- Making the entire card clickable when a detailed view exists
- Using color only to communicate meaningful status

LEVEL 4 — ANALYTICS AND CLINICAL BRIEF
Place charts, utilization data, forecasts, and the AI Clinical Brief below the action-oriented sections.

Do not place decorative analytics above urgent work.

Additional dashboard corrections:

- Reduce unnecessary vertical padding.
- Increase separation between action items and analytics.
- Ensure no text overlaps at 1440×900.
- Add loading states, empty states, and error states for each dashboard section.
- Keep filters visible without allowing them to dominate the page.
- Make the dashboard usable at 1280×720, 1440×900, and 1920×1080.

Acceptance criteria:

- A clinical or executive user can identify the three most important actions within five seconds.
- Critical alerts are visually distinct but not duplicated.
- Every alert links to a real destination.
- No card or text overlaps at supported desktop resolutions.
- Dashboard sections remain useful when there are zero alerts or no available data.
