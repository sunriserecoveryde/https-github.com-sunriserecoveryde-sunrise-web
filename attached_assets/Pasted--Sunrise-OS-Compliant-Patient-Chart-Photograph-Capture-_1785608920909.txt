# Sunrise OS — Compliant Patient Chart Photograph Capture and Identification

Complete one scoped feature:

**Allow authorized staff to take or upload a photograph of a patient and securely display it as a chart-identification photograph.**

The feature must be designed to support:

* HIPAA Privacy, Security, and Breach Notification requirements
* 42 CFR Part 2 confidentiality requirements
* Maryland Behavioral Health Administration licensing and COMAR requirements
* Maryland confidentiality and medical-record requirements
* Joint Commission Behavioral Health Care and Human Services accreditation readiness
* CARF Behavioral Health accreditation readiness
* Organizational privacy, consent, retention, and patient-rights policies

Create a new branch:

`feature/compliant-patient-chart-photo`

Do not merge into `main`.

Do not begin unrelated clinical, medication, billing, authorization, or AI work.

---

# Compliance Boundary

Do not claim that implementation of this feature alone makes Sunrise OS:

* HIPAA compliant
* 42 CFR Part 2 compliant
* Maryland BHA compliant
* Joint Commission compliant
* CARF compliant
* Legally approved for production use

The feature must provide technical controls and evidence that support compliance.

Final production approval requires:

* Privacy-officer review
* Security-officer review
* Qualified legal review
* Maryland regulatory review
* Organizational policy approval
* Clinical leadership approval
* Accreditation crosswalk review
* Staff training
* Risk analysis
* Vendor and Business Associate Agreement review
* Production security testing

Use the phrase:

**Compliance-supporting controls**

Do not use:

**Guaranteed compliant**

# 2. Treat the Photograph as Sensitive Patient Information

Treat every patient photograph as:

* Individually identifiable patient information
* Electronic protected health information when HIPAA applies
* A potentially Part 2-protected record when associated with a Part 2 program or SUD treatment
* A confidential medical-record component
* A patient-safety identification aid
* A restricted chart asset

The system must never assume that a photograph is ordinary profile imagery.

Do not use patient photographs for:

* Marketing
* Staff directories
* Public profiles
* Social media
* Facial recognition
* Facial matching
* Biometric identification
* Emotion analysis
* Age estimation
* Demographic inference
* Automated diagnosis
* Attendance surveillance
* Law-enforcement identification
* Model training
* Analytics unrelated to treatment or operations

Do not create:

* Face embeddings
* Biometric templates
* Facial-feature measurements
* Search-by-face functionality
* Automated patient identification

# 3. Production Preconditions

The feature must default to disabled in production.

Create a feature flag:

`PATIENT_CHART_PHOTO_ENABLED`

Production enablement must require validated configuration confirming:

* Real authentication is active.
* Server-side authorization is active.
* Patient-level access controls are active.
* Organization and facility isolation are active.
* Part 2 confidentiality segmentation is active where applicable.
* Private object storage is configured.
* Storage encryption is verified.
* Transport encryption is active.
* Durable audit persistence is active.
* Consent and policy configuration is active.
* Incident-response procedures exist.
* Backup and recovery cover photo metadata and storage objects.
* Approved retention policies are configured.
* Required vendor agreements have been reviewed.
* Staff training requirements are configured.

If any required production prerequisite is missing:

* Do not expose upload endpoints.
* Do not expose camera controls.
* Do not render existing photographs.
* Render a blocking configuration message for administrators.
* Do not silently enter development mode.
* Do not fall back to public or local storage.

Development may use the feature only with clearly fictitious patients and synthetic images.

# 4. Patient Rights and Organizational Policy

Create a versioned organizational policy configuration for patient photographs.

The policy must support:

* Whether patient photographs are permitted
* Whether they are optional or required by program policy
* Which programs may use them
* Which facilities may use them
* Whether an active consent or acknowledgment is required
* Whether guardian or authorized-representative approval is required
* Whether minors have additional requirements
* Whether Part 2 patients require an additional workflow
* Whether photographs may appear in patient lists
* Whether photographs may appear in printed records
* Whether photographs may be exported
* Whether replacement history is retained
* When photographs expire or require renewal
* What happens when consent is revoked
* What alternative identification process is used when the patient declines
* Retention and destruction rules

The patient must be allowed to decline a photograph when organizational policy permits refusal.

Refusal must not:

* Block necessary treatment
* Reduce quality of care
* Display stigmatizing language
* Be treated as noncompliance
* Be visible to users without a legitimate need

Support these patient-photo states:

* Not requested
* Offered
* Accepted
* Declined
* Guardian accepted
* Authorized representative accepted
* Temporarily deferred
* Consent revoked
* Photo expired
* Photo removed
* Policy exception approved

When the patient declines, show a neutral text identifier and initials.

Do not use a warning symbol suggesting that the patient is dangerous or difficult.

# 5. Consent and Authorization Workflow

Before capture or upload, evaluate the organization’s configured policy.

The workflow must determine:

* Whether a photo may be taken without a separate authorization under organizational policy
* Whether an acknowledgment is required
* Whether a HIPAA authorization is required for the intended use
* Whether a Part 2 consent or other Part 2 authority is required
* Whether a Maryland-specific consent or authorization requirement applies
* Whether the patient is a minor
* Whether a guardian or authorized representative is involved
* Whether an existing consent has expired or been revoked

Do not embed legal conclusions in frontend code.

Create a policy decision service such as:

```ts
interface PatientPhotoPolicyDecision {
  allowed: boolean;
  requiresConsent: boolean;
  requiresPart2Review: boolean;
  requiresGuardianAuthority: boolean;
  permittedUses: Array<
    "internal-identification" |
    "patient-list-thumbnail" |
    "print" |
    "export"
  >;
  retentionPolicyId: string;
  policyVersionId: string;
  denialReasonCode?: string;
}
```

The server must make the final decision.

The browser must not be able to override it.

Before upload, require staff attestation:

> I confirmed the patient’s identity using two approved identifiers, verified that this photograph belongs to the patient whose chart is open, and completed the required consent or policy workflow.

Store:

* Attestation confirmed
* Staff user ID
* Patient ID
* Episode ID where applicable
* Organization ID
* Facility ID
* Program ID
* Consent record ID where required
* Consent version
* Policy version
* Verification timestamp
* Verification method codes
* Representative authority record where applicable

Do not store the actual identifier values again in the attestation record.

---

# 6. Two-Identifier Patient Verification

A photograph must supplement patient identification.

It must never replace the required textual identification process.

Before opening the camera or uploading a photograph:

1. Display the patient’s full chart name.
2. Display two approved patient identifiers.
3. Require the staff member to confirm the correct patient.
4. Display the organization, facility, and active episode.
5. Require a second confirmation before final upload.

Approved identifiers may include, according to organizational policy:

* Full name
* Medical-record number
* Date of birth
* Another approved
