Complete one refinement task on the existing Task Group 1 branch:

**Task: Add clear trend indicators and comparison context to the Dashboard KPI cards**

Do not begin any other improvement task during this run.

Use the existing KPI cards and current dashboard data sources. Do not rebuild the Dashboard and do not change any underlying clinical, financial, or operational calculations.

## Goal

Make each KPI understandable at a glance by showing:

* Current value
* Direction of change
* Comparison period
* Whether the change is favorable, unfavorable, or neutral
* A clear route to supporting detail, when available

## KPIs to include

Apply this improvement to all existing primary KPI cards, including where currently available:

* Census
* Occupancy
* AMA Risk
* Pending Co-Signatures
* Average Length of Stay
* Discharges
* Authorizations Requiring Attention

Do not fabricate a KPI that is not currently supported by data.

## 1. Add trend indicators

Each KPI card should support one of these states:

* Increasing
* Decreasing
* No meaningful change
* Insufficient comparison data

Use:

* Up arrow for increasing
* Down arrow for decreasing
* Horizontal indicator for no meaningful change
* Neutral placeholder for insufficient data

Do not rely on color alone.

Each trend must include text such as:

* Up 6% from the previous 7 days
* Down 3 patients from the previous period
* No meaningful change
* Comparison unavailable

## 2. Show the comparison period

The comparison label must be visible or available through an accessible tooltip.

Use the most appropriate period already supported by the data:

* Previous 7 days
* Previous 30 days
* Previous calendar month
* Same period last month

Do not display a comparison period that the data does not actually support.

If historical data is unavailable, show:

**Comparison unavailable**

Do not generate fake percentages or placeholder trends.

## 3. Interpret whether the trend is favorable

Trend direction is not always inherently good or bad.

Examples:

* Census increasing may be favorable or neutral depending on capacity.
* Occupancy above the safe or target range may be unfavorable.
* AMA Risk increasing is unfavorable.
* Pending Co-Signatures increasing is unfavorable.
* Average Length of Stay may be neutral unless compared with a defined target.
* Discharges increasing may be neutral unless successful-discharge context exists.
* Authorizations Requiring Attention increasing is unfavorable.

Create a small helper function or configuration map that separates:

1. Direction
2. Operational interpretation

Use labels such as:

* Favorable
* Needs attention
* Within expected range
* Neutral
* Insufficient context

Do not change any clinical or business thresholds unless those thresholds already exist in the codebase.

## 4. Add tooltip definitions

Each KPI must have an information tooltip explaining:

* What the KPI measures
* How the displayed value is calculated
* What comparison period is being used
* What the trend indicator means

Keep tooltip language concise and non-technical.

Examples:

Census:
“Number of active patients currently admitted across the selected facility and level of care.”

Pending Co-Signatures:
“Clinical documents awaiting a required supervisory or medical co-signature.”

Do not include unsupported claims.

## 5. Make cards actionable

Where a real destination already exists, make the KPI card or a clearly labeled action open the relevant workflow.

Examples:

* Census → Open Census
* AMA Risk → Review AMA Risk
* Pending Co-Signatures → Open Co-Sign Queue
* Authorizations Requiring Attention → Review Authorizations
* Discharges → Open Discharge Activity

Requirements:

* Do not use vague labels such as “View.”
* Do not create placeholder pages.
* Preserve existing permission rules.
* If the user lacks access, do not make the card clickable.
* Do not place nested interactive buttons inside another clickable element.

## 6. Standardize the KPI card layout

Each card must use a consistent structure:

1. KPI label
2. Information tooltip
3. Current value
4. Trend indicator
5. Comparison label
6. Operational interpretation
7. Optional specific action

Design requirements:

* Consistent height across all KPI cards
* Consistent spacing and typography
* No text wrapping that breaks the layout
* No overflow at 1280×720
* Trend text must remain readable at 200% browser zoom
* Keyboard-accessible tooltips and actions
* Visible focus states

## 7. Empty and unavailable states

When a KPI value or historical comparison is unavailable:

* Do not display zero unless zero is the actual value.
* Display “Unavailable” or “Comparison unavailable.”
* Keep the card layout intact.
* Include an accessible explanation.

## Acceptance criteria

* Every displayed KPI has a current value and comparison state.
* No fake historical data is created.
* Every trend includes an icon and text.
* Trend meaning remains understandable without color.
* Favorable and unfavorable interpretations are appropriate to the KPI.
* Tooltips explain the metric and comparison period.
* Clickable cards open functioning destinations.
* Permission rules remain unchanged.
* Card heights remain consistent.
* No overlap occurs at 1280×720, 1440×900, or 1920×1080.
* TypeScript check passes.
* Production build passes.
* Existing tests pass, if configured.
